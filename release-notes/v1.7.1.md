A lighter, cleaner Frame Fang: much less memory while it waits in the tray, a tidier editor panel that answers the pointer, and a calmer capture screen.

## Changed

- **Much less memory in the background.** Frame Fang used to prepare every one of the nearly 3,900 letters in its built-in font the first time it drew a menu, and kept all of them, about 9.5 MB, for as long as it ran. It now reads a letter only when it is first drawn, which takes it from about 16 MB to about 7 MB while idle. Text is laid out exactly as before.
- **Smaller tool buttons that react to the pointer.** The editor's tool column and action row are a size smaller (30 px buttons instead of 36), so they take less room beside your picture. The button under the pointer now eases lit at once, with a lighter face and an edge in your color scheme, so it's clear what a click will hit.
- **Tips wait a moment.** A button's tip shows once the pointer has rested on it, instead of flashing up as the pointer passes. Once one is showing, moving along the panel shows the next straight away.
- **The size readout stays out of the way.** While you drag out a region, its size now sits just above the box, outside it, instead of over the corner of what you're capturing. Against the top of the screen it tucks just inside the corner.
- **A cleaner selection.** The frame is a single thin line while you drag. The small handles for resizing only show where the box can actually be pulled (picking a region to record, cropping), and they're smaller.

## New

- **Frame Fang tells you when another screenshot tool wants Print Screen (Windows).** If Lightshot, ShareX, Greenshot, Snagit, PicPick, FastStone Capture, Screenpresso or Flameshot is running while your capture key is Print Screen, Frame Fang says so once when it starts, and the Print Screen card in Settings names the tool, with the two ways out: turn Print Screen off in that tool, or pick another capture key.

## Fixed

- The highlight around your screen no longer grows in from the top left when you press Print Screen; it appears right where it belongs.

## Updating

From 1.4.0 or later, use **Check for updates** in the tray and press **Update now**: Frame Fang downloads, installs and reopens by itself.

From 1.3.0 or earlier, your copy still has the old updater, so this update goes the old way once: use **Check for updates** and follow the installer, or download `Framefang.msi` below and run it.

## Files

- `Framefang.msi`: the Windows installer.
- `Framefang-portable.exe`: Windows, runs without installing.
- `Framefang-macos-arm64.dmg`: macOS on Apple silicon (M1 and later).
- `Framefang-macos-x64.dmg`: macOS on Intel.
- `Framefang-linux-x86_64.AppImage`: Linux, one file. Make it executable, then run it.
- `Framefang-linux-x86_64-portable.tar.gz` and `Framefang-linux-arm64-portable.tar.gz`: Linux, the bare app.
- `SHA256SUMS`: checksums the in-app updater verifies.

The Mac app isn't signed by Apple yet. The first time you open it, macOS blocks it: go to **System Settings > Privacy & Security** and click **Open Anyway**.
